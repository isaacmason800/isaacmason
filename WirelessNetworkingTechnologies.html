Wireless Networking Technologies

Frequencies

2.4 GHz

  • Range and Penetration
    • Longest Range
    • Better Wall Penetration
  • Uses
    • Legacy Device Support
    • Longe range coverage
    • Environments with walls/obstacles
    • IoT Devices
  • Resistance to interferance
    • Bluetooth (Operates on same range)
    • Microwave ovens
    • Fewer channels means more congestion.
  • Channels
    • Supports both 20 MHz and 40 MHz Channel Width
    • At 20 MHz has 3 non-overlapping channels 1, 6 and 11.

5 GHz

  • Range and Penetration
    • Moderate Range
    • Moderate Wall Penetration
  • Uses
    • General Purpose Wi-Fi
    • Higher Speed applications
    • Gaming/HD streaming
  • Resistance to interferance
    • Unlike 2.4 GHz 5 GHz is not affected by Bluetooth, Microwave ovens etc
    • More channels means less overlapping channels and congestion
  • Channels
    • Supports 20 MHz, 40 MHz, 80 MHz and 160 MHz Channel Width
    • Upto 24 non-overlapping channels.

6 GHz

  • Range and Penetration
    • Due to higher frequency more path loss
    • Shortest range and penetration compated to 2.4 Ghz and 5 Ghz
  • Uses
    • High Density Environment
    • Very High Throughput Applications
    • Modern Devices Wi-Fi 6E/7
  • Resistance to interferance
    • May not support all devices meaning less congestion
    • More channels means less overlapping channels and congestion
  • Channels
    • Supports 20 MHz, 40 MHz, 80 MHz, 160 MHz and 320 MHz Channel Width
    • Upto 59 non-overlapping channels.

Regulations

Regulatory bodies decide which parts of the radio spectrum can be legally used and under what conditions, such as:

  • Frequency
  • Channels
  • Channel Widths
  • Powel Levels

These rules exist for safety reasons and to prevent interference on crtitical services
